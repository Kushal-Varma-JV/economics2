D. Answer in about 4 sentences (2 marks each)

1.Mention the Central problems of an economy.

Model Question · P4-Q17Question Bank · V.1
Answer

Every society faces scarcity of resources, and its scarce resources have competing usages. So every society has to decide how to use its scarce resources. The central problems are:

  1. What is produced and in what quantities? – how much of each of the many possible goods and services to produce.
  2. How are these goods produced? – how much of which resources to use and which technology to adopt (more labour or more machines).
  3. For whom are these goods produced? – how the produce of the economy is distributed among the individuals in the economy.

Production, exchange and consumption of goods and services are among the basic economic activities of life.

  1. Production – e.g. a family farm produces corn by using its resources.

  3. Consumption – the farm uses part of the produce for consumption purposes.

3.What is Production Possibility Frontier?

Model Question · P1-Q17Question Bank · V.3
Answer

The production possibility frontier is a curve that gives the maximum amount of one good (corn) that can be produced in the economy for any given amount of the other good (cotton), and vice-versa.

It gives the combinations of corn and cotton that can be produced when the resources of the economy are fully utilised.

Any point on or below the curve represents a combination that can be produced with the economy's resources. A point strictly below the frontier means that all or some of the resources are underemployed or are utilised in a wasteful fashion.

Model Question · P2-Q17Question Bank · V.4
Answer

A mixed economy is one where some important decisions are taken by the government and the economic activities are by and large conducted through the market. In reality, all economies are mixed economies. The only difference is in terms of the extent of the role of the government in deciding the course of economic activities.

Example: India – since Independence, the government has played a major role in planning economic activities, though its role has been reduced considerably in the last couple of decades.

7.Write the meaning of Opportunity cost with an example.

Model Question · P2-Q21
Answer

If more of the scarce resources are used in the production of one good, fewer resources are available for the production of the other good. So there is always a cost of having a little more of one good in terms of the amount of the other good that has to be forgone. This is known as the opportunity cost of an additional unit of the good. It is also called the economic cost.

Example: In an economy producing corn and cotton, moving from possibility A (0 corn, 10 cotton) to B (1 corn, 9 cotton) means 1 unit of cotton is given up to get 1 unit of corn. So the opportunity cost of that 1 unit of corn is 1 unit of cotton.

Similarly, if a family wants to have a bigger house, it may have to give up the idea of having a few more acres of arable land.

2.What are the three Central problems of an economy? Explain.

Model Question · P3-Q27Question Bank · VI.2Teacher's Notes · Q3
Answer

In an economy resources are limited and human wants are unlimited. So, the problem of choice arises. The scarce resources of an economy have competing usages. The problem of choice is called as economic problem.

Every economy, whether simple /complex, capitalist / controlled / mixed, developed / underdeveloped will have to solve the following three fundamental economic problems:

A. What to produce?

As resources are scarce an economy cannot produce everything. What goods are to be produced (Problem of guns or butter) and how much is to be produced depends on

the countries planners in socialism,
on market forces of demand and supply in a market economy.
and on both planners and market forces in a mixed economy.

B. How to produce?

This problem is relating to choice of technology there are two techniques of production.

A. Labour intensive techniques (Use more labour to produce goods)
B. Capital intensive techniques (Use more machines to produce goods)

C. For whom to produce?

Human wants and resources available vary from region to region. The people may be poor or rich. So, the important question is "for whom are these goods to be produced"? This question leads to the problem of distribution.

5.How does the Market economy work?

Model Question · P4-Q27Question Bank · VI.5
Answer

In a market economy, all economic activities are organised through the market.

A market, as studied in economics, is an institution which organises the free interaction of individuals pursuing their respective economic activities. In other words, a market is a set of arrangements where economic agents can freely exchange their endowments or products with each other.

The term 'market' in economics has nothing as such to do with a marketplace. Buyers and sellers may or may not meet in an actual physical location. They can interact in a village chowk or a super bazaar in a city, or through telephone or internet.

The arrangements which allow people to buy and sell commodities freely are the defining features of a market.

For the smooth functioning of any system, there must be coordination among the activities of its different parts; otherwise there can be chaos. In a market system, this coordination among millions of isolated individuals is brought about by prices.

In a market system, all goods or services come with a price, mutually agreed upon by the buyers and sellers, at which the exchanges take place. The price reflects, on an average, the society's valuation of the good or service.

If the buyers demand more of a certain good, the price of that good will rise. This signals to the producers that society as a whole wants more of that good than is currently being produced, and the producers are likely to increase their production.

In this way, prices of goods and services send important information to all the individuals across the market and help achieve coordination in a market system.

Thus, in a market system, the central problems regarding how much and what to produce are solved through the coordination of economic activities brought about by the price signals.

How price signals work in a market economy

Buyers demand more of a good
↓
Price of that good rises
↓
Signal to producers: society wants more of the good
↓
Producers increase production
↓
Coordination: what and how much to produce is solved
