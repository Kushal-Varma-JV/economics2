1.State the conditions needed for profit maximization by a firm under Perfect competition.

Model Question · P2-Q20Question Bank · V.1
Answer

For profits to be maximum, three conditions must hold at the output level q0:

  1. The price, p, must equal MC.
  2. Marginal cost must be non-decreasing at q0.
  3. For the firm to continue to produce, in the short run, price must be greater than or equal to the average variable cost (p ≥ AVC); in the long run, price must be greater than or equal to the average cost (p ≥ AC).

2.Give the meaning of Shut down point.

Model Question · P1-Q20Question Bank · V.2
Answer

In the short run the firm continues to produce as long as the price remains greater than or equal to the minimum of AVC. Along the supply curve, as we move down, the last price-output combination at which the firm produces positive output is the point of minimum AVC where the SMC curve cuts the AVC curve. Below this, there will be no production. This point is called the short run shut down point of the firm.

In the long run, the shut down point is the minimum of the LRAC curve.

3.Write the meaning of Opportunity cost with example.

Question Bank · V.3
Answer

Opportunity cost of some activity is the gain foregone from the second best activity.

Example: Suppose you have Rs 1,000 which you decide to invest in your family business. If you do not invest this money, you can either keep it in the house-safe which gives zero return, or deposit it in bank-1 or bank-2, which give interest at 10 per cent and 5 per cent respectively. The maximum benefit from the other alternatives is the interest from bank-1. So, the opportunity cost of investing the money in your family business is the amount of forgone interest from bank-1.

4.Mention the two determinants of a firm's Supply curve.

Model Question · P3-Q20Model Question · P5-Q20Question Bank · V.4
Answer

A firm's supply curve is a part of its marginal cost curve, so any factor that affects the marginal cost curve is a determinant of its supply curve. The two determinants are:

  1. Technological progress: It lowers the firm's marginal cost and shifts the supply curve to the right. At any given market price, the firm supplies more output.
  2. Input prices: An increase in the price of an input raises the marginal cost and shifts the supply curve to the left. At any given market price, the firm supplies less output.

5.Find out the Market supply when the supply curves of two producers are S1(p) = p – 20 and S2(p) = p – 10 respectively.

Question Bank · V.5
Answer

The market supply is the summation of the supplies of the individual firms: Sm(p) = S1(p) + S2(p)

Firm 1 produces (p – 20) when p ≥ 20 and 0 when p < 20. Firm 2 produces (p – 10) when p ≥ 10 and 0 when p < 10.

So, the market supply is:

  • Sm(p) = 0   when p < 10
  • Sm(p) = p – 10   when p ≥ 10 and p < 20
  • Sm(p) = (p – 20) + (p – 10) = 2p – 30   when p ≥ 20

1.Explain the features of Perfect competition.

Question Bank · VI.1Teacher's Notes · I.2
Answer

A. Large number of buyers and sellers: - There will be large number of buyers and sellers of a product in a perfect competitive market.

B. Homogeneous products: - The firms in a perfect competition will produce only identical or homogeneous products.

C. Single price: -Since there is large number of sellers in perfect competition, there will be single price. And no single seller or firm can change the price of a product by changing its output.

D. Freedom of entry and exit: -In a perfect competition, there is freedom of entry and exit. This means, any new firms is free to enter the industry and any existing firm is free to leave the industry.

E. Price taker: -Each firm in a perfect competition is "Price takers". This means no single firm is influence the price level by changing its output. So, each firm has zero market power.

F. Absence of transport cost: -For the existence of perfect competition the transport costs should not be considered. All the firms have equal access to the market. Price of the product is not affected by the cost of transportation of goods.

G. Free mobility of factors of production: - Another feature of the perfect competition is the goods and services as well as resources are perfectly mobile between firms. Factors of production can freely move from one occupation to another and from one firm to another. There is no barrier on their movement.

H. Absence of selling cost: -Under perfect competition there is no need of selling cost. Selling costs are the expenditure incurred to increase the sale of product. You know that under perfect competition goods are completely homogeneous. When they cannot change the price and when their goods are completely similar, firms need not make any expenditure and publicity and advertisement.

I. Normal profit: - The firm is a perfect competition will be earning normal profit. The normal profit is that just sufficient to stay in the market.

J. Perfect market information: - All the buyers and sellers in a perfect competition have perfect market information.

So, these are the main features of the perfect competitive market, but it is clear from the above features that in perfect competitive market individual firms have zero market power and this form of markets exists really.

5.Explain the determinants of a firm's supply curve.

Model Question · P1-Q29Question Bank · VI.5Teacher's Notes · I.1
Answer

A. Technological progress: - Improvement in technology may reduce the total cost and the marginal cost of the product. Which leads to increase in quantity of production and supply may increase. Using outdated technology may result in less supply.

B. Input prices: -If the price of an input, say wage rate of labor increases, the marginal cost and total cost may also increase resulting in upward shift of the supply curve. Decreasing price of inputs leads to increase in supply.

C. Tax policy of the government: -If government increases taxes the marginal cost of the production increases and the supply curve moves upwards to the left and the firm supplies fewer units of output. If the governments reduce the tax supply is going to be increases.

D. Goal of the firm: - If a firm's goal is profit maximization, then the firm may supply more and tries to maximize profit. On the other hand, if the firm's goal is risk minimization it may produce less and supply less.

E. Nature of the market: -In monopoly, a monopolist may restrict supply to raise the price and earn more profit. In perfect competitive market, there will be no tendency to restrict supply. As the number of firms increases supply also increases.

F. Expectation about the future prices: - If a producer expects an increase in market price in future, he will supply less today and hoard the stock to sell at a higher price in future and vice versa.

G. Climatic conditions: - favorable climatic conditions may increase agricultural production and supply may also increase. On the other hand, unfavorable climatic conditions reduce the supply of agricultural commodities and its allied goods.

H. Prices of the products: - If the final goods price increases the supply is also going to be increase to earn more profit and vice versa.

2.Explain the profit maximization of a firm under the following conditions. a) P = MC   b) MC must be non-decreasing at q0

Question Bank · VII.2Teacher's Notes · II.5
Answer

(a)P=MC

Profits are the difference between total revenue and total cost. Both total revenue and total cost increase as output increases. Notice that as long as the change in total revenue is greater than the change in total cost, profits will continue to increase. Recall that change in total revenue per unit increase in output is the marginal revenue; and the change in total cost per unit increase in output is the marginal cost. Therefore, we can conclude that as long as marginal revenue is greater than marginal cost, profits are increasing. By the same logic, as long as marginal revenue is less than marginal cost, profits will fall. It follows that for profits to be maximum, marginal revenue should equal marginal cost.

In other words, profits are maximum at the level of output (which we have called q0) for which MR = MC For the perfectly competitive firm, we have established that the MR = P. So the firm's profit maximizing output becomes the level of output at which P=MC.

(b)MC must be none decreasing= at Q0

DIAGRAM

EXPLANATION

Conditions 1 and 2 for profit maximization.

Conditions 1 and 2 for profit maximization. The figure is used to demonstrate that when the market price is p, the output level of a profit maximizing firm cannot be q1 (marginal cost curve, MC, is downward sloping), q2 and q3 (market price exceeds marginal cost), or q4 and q5 (marginal cost exceeds market price).

Here the output level q1, the market price is equal to the marginal cost; however, the marginal cost curve is downward sloping. We claim that q1 cannot be a profit maximizing output level.

Observe that for all output levels slightly to the left of q1, for q2 the market price is lower than the marginal cost, for q4 and q5 MC is more than market price. The argument implies that the firm's profit at an output level slightly smaller than q1 exceeds that corresponding to the output level q1 . This being the case, q1 cannot be a profit-maximizing output level.

2.Give the meaning of TR, MR and AR. (For Blind students only)

Question Bank · VIII (Blind students)
Answer

Total Revenue (TR): Total revenue of a firm is defined as the market price of the good (p) multiplied by the firm's output (q). TR = p × q

Marginal Revenue (MR): The marginal revenue of a firm is defined as the increase in total revenue for a unit increase in the firm's output. MR = Change in total revenue / Change in quantity

Average Revenue (AR): The average revenue of a firm is defined as total revenue per unit of output. AR = TR / q = p

For a price-taking firm, MR = AR = p (market price).
